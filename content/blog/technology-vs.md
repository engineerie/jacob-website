There is on the other hand a call for using actual horses in the forest. The benefit is that it is lighter on the ground, it doesn't disturb and compact the ground as much. However, it's heavy- physical. It's also slow and you need a horse. However, being nimble and able to walk through the forest without damaging it too much, speaks to another thing around precision forestry. 

In a conversation with Karin Öhman, she poked out that in 20 years 20% of the Swedish forest will be harvested by drones. 

Air forestry have recently done a test flight 

> The company is specifically targeting hard-to-reach areas where conventional forestry machinery is either inefficient or environmentally damaging. According to AirForestry, the drones are capable of operating in harsh conditions, including snow, rain, temperatures down to -20°C, and wind gusts of up to 13 m/s

It seems they can then finally cut down the hard to reach areas, like the last remaining highland forest.
